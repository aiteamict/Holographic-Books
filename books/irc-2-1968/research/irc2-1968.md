## 1. Introduction

- 1.1 Type designs for the NH route marker were developed in the Roads Wing, Ministry of Transport, Government of
  India; discussed at the Chief Engineers' meeting, April 1952; issued by the Consulting Engineer (Road Development)
  for general adoption and published as an IRC Standard in 1953.
- 1.2 Switch to the metric system made it necessary to metricise the Standard. Considered by an IRC subcommittee,
  then by the Specifications and Standards Committee (meetings in 1967), with a general revision. Approved by the
  Executive Committee on 24 September 1968 and by the Council at Bombay on 2 November 1968.

## 2. Design

- 2.1 The sign is a **shield painted on a rectangular plate 450 mm × 600 mm**. Design in Plate 1.
- 2.2 **Yellow background; lettering and border black.** Yellow = "Canary Yellow, Indian Standard Colour No. 309".
  Paint materials per Indian Standards Institution requirements.
- 2.3 Size, shape and spacing of letters and numerals per Fig. 1 and Plates 1 and 5.

### Fig. 1 — Standard letters N and H of 100 mm height

- Letter height 100. Stroke width 16 (both letters).
- N: overall width 67; the diagonal meets the verticals with a 24-high flat at the top-left / bottom-right.
- H: overall width 67; crossbar centred 44 from the bottom; inner clear width between stems = 67 − 2×16 = 35;
  a "14" dimension marks the stem-to-bar join detail.
- Gap between N and H = 45.

## 3. Location

- 3.1 Erected on National Highways (a) ahead of intersections with other important roads, (b) immediately after
  intersections as **confirmatory** route markers, (c) at suitable locations through built-up areas, (d) at other
  points needed to guide through traffic.
- 3.2 Erected as in Plate 2 ("Arrangement for Erection of National Highway Route Marker Signs").
- 3.3 **Roads without kerbs: 2 to 3 m clear between the post and the edge of the carriageway.**
  **Roads with kerbs: not less than 60 cm from the edge of the kerb** (text says "60 mm" — a misprint; Plate 2 says
  "≮ 60 cm"; use 60 cm). To avoid specular reflection the sign is **turned slightly away from the road** (Plate 2:
  "Positioning of the sign" — face set at a little over 90° to the road edge).
- 3.4 Distance along the NH from the junction, either side: **100 to 150 m**. Fixed on the **left-hand side** as one
  approaches the junction.
- Plate 2 plan: the confirmatory marker is **15 to 30 m** after the junction (also left-hand side, after the kerb
  radius R).

## 4. Definition plate

- 4.1 When erected in advance of a junction, the direction the NH takes at the junction is shown on a **definition
  plate 300 mm × 250 mm fixed below the shield** (Plate 2).
- 4.2 Background colour same as the shield (Canary Yellow IS 309); border and arrow black.
- 4.3 Type designs of arrows in Plate 3.

## 5. Route marker assembly at junctions with numbered routes

- 5.1 Where a numbered route intersects or takes off from a NH, its route marker may be erected ahead of the
  intersection **on the same post** as the NH marker being travelled on, each with a definition plate carrying a
  single or double-headed arrow pointing in the direction(s) that route may be followed.
- 5.2 Two examples in Plate 4.

## 6. Colour of back of sign and post

- Reverse of all route marker signs: **unobtrusive grey, Indian Standard Colour No. 630**.
- Post painted in **25 cm bands, alternately black and white, lowest band (next to the ground) black**.

## 7. Materials

- Enamelled or painted steel plate.

## Plate 1 — National Highway Route Marker Sign (page 5)

Rectangular plate 450 × 600, all four corners R = 12.

Shield border: black, **10 thick**, set **12 in from the plate edge** on both sides and at the top; the shield's
top edge has a shallow **V notch at centre** (dip of about 2, over 25 each side of centre, "2 11" detail).

Vertical zoning (top → bottom): 12 margin · **75** · **100 (letters N H)** · **75** · **200 (numerals)** · **126** · 12
= 600. Letters N and H centred with a 45 gap. The numeral zone is 200 high (the "20 cm numerals" of Plate 5).

Shield outline (construction drawing, right of Plate 1): straight sides for **360** below the top (after a 25 top
strip), then a **215**-high curved bottom. The bottom curve is drawn with arcs: **R 318** struck from points 100 in from
each side at the top of the curved zone, and a central **R 132** arc at the very bottom; the sides join the curve
with **R 120** fillets; a **R 12** at the bottom centre. Two **mounting holes** at the top, 110 either side of centre
(220 apart), and two at the bottom likewise. The bottom of the plate below the shield point is 25.

Scale bar: 60 30 0 60 120 180 240 mm (right drawing) and 30 15 0 30 60 90 120 150 180 210 mm (left drawing).

Labels: LETTERS BLACK · BORDER BLACK · BACKGROUND CANARY YELLOW (I.S. COLOUR NO. 309) · NUMERALS BLACK.
Example numeral shown: **NH 42**.

## Plate 2 — Arrangement for Erection (page 7)

Assembly (elevation):
- Shield plate 450 × 600 with **13 mm diameter holes for 10 mm diameter bolts**, holes 50 apart about the post centre
  line, 25 below the top edge (two at top, two at bottom, 50 above bottom edge).
- Vertical: 25 · 62 · 100 · 75 · 200 · 126 · 12 (= 600) · gap **150** · **definition plate 250 high × 300 wide** ·
  **1250** to crown level of road. **Top of shield = 2250 above crown level of road.**
- Post: **75 mm × 75 mm × 10 mm T-iron** "or any other suitable arrangement".
- Scale: 50 25 0 50 100 150 200 250 mm.

Plan "Location of route marker sign at a junction" (a T-junction / fork):
- Distance **D = 100 m to 150 m** from the junction (measured to the kerb line R).
- Confirmatory route marker **15 to 30 m** after the junction on each leg.
- 1. Roads with kerbs — **≮ 60 cm** from the edge of the kerb.
- 2. Roads without kerbs — **2 to 3 m** from the edge of the carriageway.
- "Positioning of the sign": the face is at 90° + a small angle to the carriageway edge, offset d from the edge
  (turned slightly away from traffic).

## Plate 3 — Details of typical arrows for use on the definition plate (page 9)

Plate 300 wide × 250 high; black border **10** thick set **15** in from the plate edge; background Canary Yellow.
Shaft width **45** everywhere; arrowhead **90** high, **50** half-base (100 wide), head tips rounded **R 8**, 70 from
tip to shoulder; shaft corners R 8 outer / R 4 inner on the turn arrow. Six example arrows (left→right, top→bottom):

1. **Straight ahead** (up): tip 25 below border; a horizontal crossbar 12 thick, 130 wide, 25 above the shaft foot
   (the "road" bar) — shaft foot 15 above the border.
2. **Left turn**: shaft enters from bottom-right (45 wide, 25 from border), turns left (R 8 outer, R 4 inner), head
   points left, tip 25 from left border, head centred vertically.
3. **Double-headed diagonal** (↗ and ↙): heads at 25 from top-right and bottom-left corners, shaft 45.
4. **Ahead-right diagonal with stem**: a short vertical stem from the bottom (80 high, 60 from the left), 45 wide,
   then a diagonal to a head 25 from the top-right, with a 12-thick crossbar at the base (60 wide).
5. **Straight right** (→): shaft from the left border (15 in), head tip 25 from the right border, centred vertically.
6. **Ahead-left diagonal** (↖): shaft from lower-right (25 from border) to a head 25 from the top-left corner.

Notes: 1. All dimensions in millimetres. 2. Background Canary Yellow (IS Colour No. 309), border and arrow black.
3. The arrows are examples only; for other situations arrows may be evolved on similar lines.

## Plate 4 — Route marker assembly at junctions of NHs with other numbered routes (page 11)

Example 1 — crossroads of **NH 6** (east–west) and **NH 7** (north–south). On every approach, **100 to 150 m** before
the junction: a two-shield assembly on one post — the shield of the road being travelled with a **straight-ahead**
arrow plate, and the crossing road's shield with a **double-headed (↔)** arrow plate; **15 to 30 m** after the
junction on every exit: a single confirmatory shield of that road.

Example 2 — **NH 26** takes off from **NH 7** (Y-junction). On NH 7 approaching from the south: assembly NH 26 (arrow
ahead-left ↖) + NH 7 (straight ahead ↑); from the north: NH 26 + NH 7; on NH 26 approaching: NH 7 (double-headed,
turn both ways); then confirmatory single shields 15–30 m after on each leg.

Note: "The examples are for the case when the intersecting route is a National Highway. The route marker assembly
will basically be the same when the intersecting route is a State route or of some other classification, the only
difference being in the shield."
